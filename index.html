What I Treat

Neurological rehabilitation

My postgraduate training is in neurosciences, and most of my caseload sits here. Every case starts with an assessment before I decide a plan.

🧠

Stroke Rehabilitation

Rebuilding movement and independence after a stroke, from bed mobility to walking.

  • Sitting, standing and transfers
  • Use of the affected arm and hand
  • Spasticity management
⚕️

Neurological Conditions

Long-term and progressive conditions, managed to hold on to function for as long as possible.

  • Parkinson's disease
  • Multiple sclerosis
  • Spinal cord injuries
  • Peripheral Nerve injuries
  • Neuropathies
  • Bell's palsy, Guillain–Barré syndrome
👶

Paediatric Rehabilitation

Supporting children to develop movement, strength, coordination, and independence through age-appropriate rehabilitation.

  • Cerebral palsy
  • Developmental delay
  • Paediatric neurological conditions
  • Gait, balance and coordination difficulties
🚶

Balance & Walking

For patients who are unsteady, have had a fall, or are relearning to walk after illness or surgery.

  • Gait retraining
  • Fall-prevention work
  • Choosing and using walking aids
I also treat post-operative recovery (knee and hip replacement, spine surgery), orthopaedic and back pain, and elderly patients who find it hard to travel to a clinic.

About

Trained in neurosciences, focused on function

I completed my Master of Physiotherapy in Neurosciences at Dr. D. Y. Patil College, Pune. The specialisation covers motor control, motor learning, balance and coordination, and what happens to each of them after a stroke or spinal injury, or in conditions such as Parkinson's disease.

Neurological recovery is slower and less linear than orthopaedic recovery, and it depends heavily on what the patient practises between sessions. That is the reason I treat people in their own surroundings: bed height, stair depth, bathroom layout and doorway width all decide what is actually possible to rehearse, and a plan built around them holds up better than one written for a treatment room.

I've completed more than 50 sessions and I'm currently taking new patients, including complex cases. If you aren't sure whether physiotherapy will help, message me before booking anything.

Is the first visit really free?

Yes. The first visit is a full assessment of 45 to 60 minutes at no charge, with no obligation to book anything afterwards. You'll get my honest view on whether physiotherapy will help, roughly how long it would take, and what it would cost. One free assessment per patient, within the areas listed above.

What do sessions cost after that?

₹800 to ₹1,000 per session, depending on the condition and your area. That covers specialist neurological treatment in a full 45 to 60 minute session. Packages of 10 sessions or more are charged at a reduced rate, travel is included within the areas listed above, and there are no hidden charges.

Do I need a doctor's referral?

No. You can contact me directly. If you have recent reports, scans or a discharge summary, keep them ready for the first visit. They help me plan more accurately.

How long is a session, and how many will we need?

Sessions run 45 to 60 minutes, and the first visit takes a little longer. How many you need depends on the condition and how long ago it started. I'll give you an estimate and a set of goals after assessing, and I'll say so if physiotherapy isn't the right answer.

Do you bring equipment?

Yes, I carry what a session needs. If something is required longer term, such as a walker or resistance bands, I'll advise on what to buy and from where.

What are your timings?

Monday to Saturday, 8:00 AM to 7:00 PM. Sunday visits by prior arrangement.
